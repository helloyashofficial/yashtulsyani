01 · The question

One problem, underneath everything

A fear about a person can produce the behaviour that brings about the thing feared. Insecurity becomes a need for reassurance. Reassurance becomes an expectation. Expectation becomes pressure. Pressure changes how the other person behaves, and then the person who was afraid points at the result and says they knew all along.

Nothing supernatural happens in that sequence. A prediction is made and then acted on until it comes true. Everything I write is some version of that problem, looked at from psychology, from philosophy, or from the physics of time.

The interesting part is not that we are wrong about each other. It is that being wrong about each other is a mechanism, and mechanisms can be interrupted.

03 · The rule

Where the physics stops

This subject attracts a particular kind of writing, so the position is stated plainly and early rather than buried in a note at the bottom. I do not think consciousness creates physical reality. I do not think two people are entangled in the sense a physicist means. I do not think quantum mechanics shows that wanting something makes it happen.

Where I use the language of physics about human life, I am borrowing a structure of thought, and I say so at the point of borrowing. It rules out the more thrilling version of every argument here, which I think is a good trade. There is more on that on the about page →.
